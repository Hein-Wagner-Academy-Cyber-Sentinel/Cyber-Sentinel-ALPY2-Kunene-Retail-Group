**Kunene Retail Group — Business Profile**

**1. Business Overview**

**Business name:** Kunene Retail Group  
**Business type:** Multi-branch retailer  
**Project persona:** Simulated South African business  
**Project pair:** Pair 2

Kunene Retail Group is a growing multi-branch retailer that is moving from traditional in-store tills towards **e-commerce and card payments**.

The project brief identifies Kunene’s main security risk areas as:

-   Payment card data
-   Distributed store endpoints
-   Seasonal increases in demand

The purpose of this project is to design a secure, code-defined platform that supports the business while protecting its information and maintaining availability.

**2. Business Transformation**

Kunene is moving from mainly in-store transactions towards online shopping and electronic card payments.

This change creates additional technology and security requirements because the business will need to protect:

-   Customer information
-   Online accounts
-   Orders and transactions
-   Payment-related information
-   Store endpoint systems
-   Employee and administrator accounts
-   Business and inventory information

The move to e-commerce also increases the number of systems and users that may interact with the business environment.

**3. Important Assets**

The following assets are considered important to Kunene’s environment:

| **Asset**                          | **Why it matters**                                                                  |
|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Customer personal information      | Must be protected from unauthorised access or disclosure                            |
| Payment-related information        | Could cause serious financial and security consequences if compromised              |
| E-commerce platform                | Required for online sales and customer access                                       |
| Store endpoint systems             | Used across multiple branches and increase the attack surface                       |
| User accounts and credentials      | Could be abused to gain unauthorised access                                         |
| Orders and transaction information | Important for business operations and customer service                              |
| Inventory information              | Needed to support retail operations                                                 |
| System availability                | Customers must be able to access the online service, especially during busy periods |

**4. Stakeholders**

The main stakeholders considered in this project are:

-   Customers
-   Store employees
-   Branch management
-   IT and security personnel
-   Business management
-   Payment and technology service providers
-   Product Vendors

Some of these stakeholders are project assumptions because the project brief does not provide Kunene’s actual organisational structure.

**5. Growth Pressures**

Kunene’s move to e-commerce creates several pressures:

1.  More online transactions
2.  More customer information being processed
3.  More systems and endpoints to secure
4.  More opportunities for attackers to target the business
5.  Increased demand during seasonal periods
6.  A greater need for reliable and available services

The security platform therefore needs to support growth without creating unnecessary security weaknesses.

**6. Security Priorities**

The main security priorities for Kunene are:

-   Protect personal information
-   Protect payment-related information
-   Control access to systems and data
-   Secure distributed store endpoints
-   Protect customer and employee accounts
-   Maintain the availability of the e-commerce platform
-   Detect and respond to security incidents
-   Keep security evidence in an accessible, text-based format
-   Apply POPIA-related security requirements
-   Avoid storing unnecessary sensitive information

**7. POPIA Relevance**

POPIA is relevant because Kunene’s e-commerce and retail activities are expected to involve personal information.

Examples may include customer names, contact details, delivery information and information associated with customer transactions.

The project will therefore consider privacy and security requirements when designing the platform.

POPIA requires organisations processing personal information to meet conditions for lawful processing and to apply appropriate safeguards to protect personal information.

**8. Project Assumptions**

The following are assumptions made for the project because the project brief does not provide Kunene’s detailed technical environment:

-   Kunene operates more than one physical retail branch.
-   Kunene will use an online retail platform.
-   Customers will interact with the business through online services.
-   Store endpoints will connect to business systems.
-   The business will use electronic payment services.
-   The business will experience periods of increased seasonal demand.
-   The project environment will use simulated data rather than real customer or payment information.

These assumptions may be refined as the project develops.

**9. Out of Scope**

The following are outside the scope of this business profile:

-   Accessing real Kunene Retail Group systems
-   Using real customer information
-   Using real payment-card information
-   Using real passwords or credentials
-   Making changes to a real production environment
-   Providing a legal opinion on POPIA compliance

**10. Summary**

Kunene Retail Group is a simulated South African multi-branch retailer modernising from in-store tills towards e-commerce and card payments.

Its main security concerns are **payment card data, distributed store endpoints and seasonal load**.

The security design must therefore focus on protecting information, controlling access, securing endpoints, maintaining availability and applying appropriate privacy and security safeguards.
